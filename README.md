# Characterizing the spatiotemporal expansion of high cell density Pseudomonas aeruginosa communities in variable gel environments using swim plate assays.

Opportunistic colonization and recurring infection by Pseudomonas aeruginosa are substantial risks to lung functionality for people with underlying respiratory diseases such as cystic fibrosis and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. The complex metabolic and phenotypic adaptations P. aeruginosa exhibits in response to lung environment conditions make relevant in vitro models of pathogenic populations crucial for identifying and evaluating effective antimicrobial targets. However, an extracellular component that is rarely integrated in these experiment platforms is a spatially extensive, semisolid gel interface representing the lower respiratory tract mucus layers that P. aeruginosa propagate through via active bacterial motility. In this investigation, we examine the applicability of swim plate assays, a qualitative methodology for measuring flagellar swimming motility, as an in vitro platform to study the spatiotemporal development of PA14 in gel conditions. We observed higher extracellular concentrations and production yield of the phenazine cytotoxin pyocyanin from PA14 populations when grown in swim plate assays compared to liquid culture supporting the emergence of heterogeneous growth environments within the microbial population. To quantitatively characterize fluctuations to population-scale spatiotemporal expansion in response to gel composition alterations, we developed complementing timelapse microscopy and continuum mechanics modeling workflows to measure dynamic, fundamental changes in PA14 swimming motility and growth kinetics. Workflow results determined that the presence of 0.30% w/v of native MUC5AC lung mucins had a confining effect on PA14 swimming motility that was more extensive than free volume reduction in the swim plate gel resulting in lower spatial spreading of the population. Additionally, the decrease to PA14 motility led to sharper accumulation of localized biomass density in MUC5AC incorporated swim plate assays and high cell density growth conditions over a more extended experimental time span. Combined with this experiment platform’s resource accessibility, the swim plate assay as an in vitro model can enable investigations of pathogenic community dynamics in gel conditions over more extensive spatial and time scales.
